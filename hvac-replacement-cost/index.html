HVAC Replacement Cost: 2026 Homeowner Price Guide

✓ Verified Pricing National Turnkey Average: $6,700 – $12,500 Updated: 2026

Replacing a complete heating and cooling system costs an average of $8,500 in the United States, with typical projects ranging from $6,700 to $12,500 for standard residential applications. High-end multi-stage or modulating systems installed in large homes can range between $14,000 and $19,000+.

Entry Level (14-15 SEER2)
$6,700 - $8,200

Single-stage AC paired with an 80% AFUE gas furnace or standard air handler.

Mid-Tier (16-17 SEER2)
$8,200 - $11,800

Two-stage AC condenser with a 92%–96% AFUE furnace and ECM variable-speed blower.

Premium Inverter (18-22+ SEER2)
$11,800 - $17,500

Variable-speed modulating compressor, smart communicating thermostat, and maximum efficiency.

Cost to Replace Furnace and AC at the Same Time

Contractors strongly recommend replacing both the air conditioning unit and furnace concurrently. Because both systems share the indoor air handler, blower motor, electrical connections, and ductwork plenum, replacing both at the same time reduces redundant labor.

Itemized Cost Breakdown: What You Pay For

A transparent contractor quote will clearly break down hardware, labor, and ancillary components:

  • Equipment Wholesale Cost (45%–55% of Total): Includes the outdoor condenser, matched cased indoor evaporator coil, and gas furnace or air handler.
  • Labor & Installation (35%–45% of Total): Professional installation requires two technicians for 8 to 14 hours. Tasks include recovering old refrigerant, brazing new lines with nitrogen purge, pulling a deep vacuum (below 500 microns), electrical whip wiring, flue venting, and testing static pressure.
  • Permits & Inspections ($150 – $450): Mandatory local municipal mechanical permits to ensure safety code compliance.
  • Refrigerant Line Set Flushing or Replacement ($350 – $900): Necessary when upgrading from R-22 or contaminated lines to modern R-410A / R-454B systems.
