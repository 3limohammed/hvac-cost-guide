What Size AC Do I Need? (2026 Sizing Guide)

✓ ACCA Manual J Standards Tonnage Benchmark: 1 Ton per 400 – 600 Sq. Ft.

Residential air conditioner sizing is measured in tons (1 ton = 12,000 BTU/hr of cooling). Installing the correct tonnage is critical: an undersized unit cannot keep up during heat waves, while an oversized unit causes high indoor humidity, mold growth, and short-cycling.

Why "Bigger is Better" is a Dangerous HVAC Myth

Contractors without engineering training often recommend installing a larger unit "just to be safe." Oversizing an air conditioner is a catastrophic mistake:

  • Clammy, Humid Air: An air conditioner has two jobs: lowering air temperature (sensible cooling) and removing moisture (latent cooling). To remove humidity, the evaporator coil must run continuously for at least 15 to 20 minutes. An oversized unit drops the thermostat by 3 degrees in 6 minutes and turns off, leaving indoor relative humidity above 65%, creating an ideal breeding ground for mold and dust mites.
  • Excessive Wear & High Bills: Starting up an AC requires 5 to 7 times more electrical current than running continuously. Frequent short-cycling spikes electric bills and burns out capacitors and contactors prematurely.
  • Frozen Coils: Oversized systems push more cold air through ducts than they were designed to handle, causing airflow restriction, pressure drops, and frozen evaporator coils.

What is an ACCA Manual J Load Calculation?

The only scientifically valid method to size an HVAC system is an ACCA Manual J calculation. It factors in:

  • Total window area and Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC) ratings.
  • Attic insulation R-value (e.g. R-19 vs. R-49).
  • Building envelope air leakage (air changes per hour).
  • Home geographic orientation (west-facing windows absorb massive afternoon heat).
  • 99% summer outdoor design temperature established by ASHRAE weather stations.

Engineering References

  • ACCA Manual J (Residential Load Calculation, 8th Edition).
  • ASHRAE Fundamentals Handbook (Climatic Design Information).
