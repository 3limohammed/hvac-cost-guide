Furnace Replacement Cost: 2026 Homeowner Guide

✓ Verified Heating Data Average Installed Cost: $2,900 – $5,800 Updated: 2026

The average cost to replace a residential gas furnace ranges from $2,900 to $5,800, with most homeowners spending about $4,200 for a 95% AFUE two-stage unit installed with permits and existing venting.

Furnace Replacement Costs by Fuel Type

Furnace Type Efficiency Range (AFUE) Lifespan Installed Cost Range
Natural Gas (Standard 80%) 80% AFUE 15 – 20 Years $2,400 – $3,800
Natural Gas (High-Efficiency 92–96%) 92% – 96% AFUE 15 – 22 Years $3,400 – $5,600
Natural Gas (Modulating 97–98%) 97% – 98.5% AFUE 18 – 25 Years $5,200 – $7,900
Electric Furnace 100% AFUE (High Electric Bill) 20 – 30 Years $2,000 – $4,200
Oil Furnace 80% – 87% AFUE 20 – 25 Years $4,500 – $7,800

80% Standard vs. 96%+ High-Efficiency Condensing Furnaces

The primary decision when buying a gas furnace is whether to install an 80% standard furnace or a 90%+ high-efficiency condensing unit:

  • Standard 80% AFUE: 20 cents of every dollar spent on gas exits through the metal chimney as exhaust heat. Uses existing metal B-vent flue pipes. Best suited for mild climates (Texas, Georgia) where heating demand is low.
  • High-Efficiency 90%–98% AFUE: Features a secondary stainless steel heat exchanger that captures latent heat from flue condensation. Exhaust gas is cool enough to vent via PVC pipe through a sidewall. Reduces annual gas heating bills by 15%–25%. Recommended for cold northern climates (Michigan, Ohio, New York, Pennsylvania).

Inflation Reduction Act Tax Credit

Gas furnaces achieving an ENERGY STAR rating with at least 97% AFUE qualify for a $600 federal tax credit under Section 25C. Check your contractor quote to confirm the model meets CEE Tier 1 requirements.
