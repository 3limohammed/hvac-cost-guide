2026 Heat Pump Tax Credits & Rebates: Save Up to $2,000 – $8,000

✓ Federal Incentive Verified Federal 25C Credit: Up to $2,000 State HEEHRA Rebates: Up to $8,000

Quick Summary: Under the federal Inflation Reduction Act, homeowners installing qualifying electric heat pumps can claim an annual 30% tax credit up to $2,000 under IRS Section 25C. Furthermore, low-to-moderate-income families can stack point-of-sale state rebates up to $8,000 through the HOMES and HEEHRA programs.

How the Federal 25C Tax Credit Works

The Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (Section 25C) was substantially upgraded by the Inflation Reduction Act. Key rules every homeowner should know:

  • 30% of Total Project Cost: Unlike previous iterations that capped labor, the 30% credit applies to both the equipment purchase wholesale cost and professional contractor installation labor.
  • $2,000 Annual Cap on Heat Pumps: Heat pumps enjoy a higher $2,000 annual cap compared to standard central air conditioners and gas furnaces (which are capped at $600).
  • Annual Reset (No Lifetime Limit): The credit resets every tax year through December 31, 2032. If you install a central heat pump this year and replace your water heater or add a ductless mini-split next year, you can claim the $2,000 benefit in both years.
  • Principal Residence Requirement: The home must be an existing residential property located in the United States used as your primary residence. (Rentals and new construction do not qualify for 25C).

Efficiency Requirements: Which Heat Pumps Qualify?

Not all heat pumps qualify for the federal tax credit. The IRS mandates that systems meet the Consortium for Energy Efficiency (CEE) highest tier specifications established for your climate zone:

State-Administered HEEHRA Rebates: Up to $8,000 Off

Under the High-Efficiency Electric Home Rebate Act (HEEHRA), the Department of Energy awarded $4.3 billion to individual states. Unlike tax credits which you receive months later when filing taxes, HEEHRA provides instant point-of-sale discounts applied directly to your contractor invoice:

  • Low-Income (Under 80% Area Median Income): Covers 100% of costs up to an $8,000 maximum for a heat pump space heater.
  • Moderate-Income (80% to 150% Area Median Income): Covers 50% of costs up to an $8,000 maximum.
  • Electrical Panel Upgrades: Up to an additional $4,000 rebate if your home requires a 200-amp service upgrade to power the heat pump.

Step-by-Step: How to Claim Your Tax Credit

  1. Request an AHRI Certified Reference Sheet: When getting bids, require that your HVAC contractor provides the official AHRI Certified Reference Number proving the matched condenser and indoor coil meet CEE Tier 1 or Tier 2 ratings.
  2. Retain Itemized Contractor Invoices: Keep all paid invoices detailing equipment model numbers, serial numbers, labor costs, and the date the system was placed into service.
  3. Complete IRS Form 5695: When preparing your federal tax return, fill out Part II of IRS Form 5695 (Residential Energy Efficient Property) and transfer the calculation to Schedule 3 on Form 1040.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 2026 federal tax credit for a heat pump?

Under Internal Revenue Code Section 25C, homeowners can claim a nonrefundable tax credit of 30% of total equipment and installation costs, capped at a maximum of $2,000 per year for qualifying electric air-source heat pumps.

Can I claim the $2,000 heat pump tax credit more than once?

Yes. Unlike the old lifetime caps, Section 25C resets annually through tax year 2032. For example, you can claim $2,000 for a space-heating heat pump in 2026 and another $2,000 for a heat pump water heater or second system in 2027.

What SEER2 and HSPF2 ratings qualify for the 25C tax credit?

To qualify, ducted split heat pumps must meet or exceed CEE Tier 1 requirements (typically >= 15.2 SEER2, >= 10.0 EER2, and >= 8.1 HSPF2). In northern regions, cold-climate certified heat pumps must also meet minimum heating output at 5°F.
