Ductwork Replacement Cost: 2026 Linear Foot Guide

✓ Verified Benchmark Average Replacement Range: $1,800 – $5,200 Linear Foot Rate: $12 – $25 / ft

The average cost to replace residential ductwork ranges from $1,800 to $5,200, with most homeowners spending approximately $3,600 for a full ductwork replacement in an existing 1,800 to 2,400 sq. ft. home. Installed costs range between $12 and $25 per linear foot depending on duct material (flexible ducting vs. rigid sheet metal), attic/crawlspace accessibility, and return plenum fabrication.

Ductwork Replacement Cost by Project Scope

Project Scope Typical Linear Footage Description Installed Cost Range
Minor Duct Repair & Sealing Partial / Target Runs Mastic paste or Aeroseal sealing of joint leaks $400 – $1,200
Partial Duct Run Replacement 40 – 80 linear ft. Replacing 3 to 5 damaged, crushed, or moldy branch runs $1,200 – $2,400
Full Replacement (Existing Chases) 150 – 300 linear ft. Complete supply & return replacement in attic/crawlspace $2,400 – $5,800
Brand-New Installation (No Ducts) 200 – 400 linear ft. Full ductwork in older home including drywall framing & soffits $6,500 – $14,000+

Cost Breakdown: Labor vs. Materials

Replacing ductwork is labor-intensive, often performed in extreme attic heat or cramped crawlspaces:

Cost Component Share of Total Bill Typical Cost Work Performed
Duct Materials & Fittings 28% – 35% $800 – $1,600 R-6/R-8 flexible duct, sheet metal trunk, boots, dampers
Attic & Crawlspace Labor 52% – 62% $1,500 – $3,200 Demolition of old ducts, hanging new runs, strapping
Sealing, Balancing & Permits 10% – 15% $300 – $700 Mastic sealing, airflow static balancing, municipal inspection

Material Comparison: Flexible Duct vs. Rigid Sheet Metal

  • Flexible Insulated Duct (Flex Duct): Costs $12 to $18 per linear foot installed. Constructed of a wire coil wrapped in fiberglass insulation (R-6 or R-8) and a vapor barrier. Flex duct is affordable and quiet, but must be installed taut without sags, sharp 90-degree kinks, or pinching.
  • Rigid Galvanized Sheet Metal: Costs $18 to $30 per linear foot installed. Exceptionally durable (lasts 35+ years), smooth internal surface delivers superior airflow with minimal friction loss, and cannot be chewed through by rodents. Requires external fiberglass insulation wrapping.

ACCA Manual D: Why Airflow Sizing Matters

Contractors must design duct systems in accordance with ACCA Manual D (Residential Duct Design). Proper airflow requires:

  • Static Pressure: Total external static pressure (ESP) should generally not exceed 0.50 inches water column (in. w.g.). Undersized ducts cause high static pressure, choking airflow, overheating furnace heat exchangers, and causing compressor motors to overheat.
  • CFM per Ton: Standard air conditioning and heat pump systems require 350 to 400 Cubic Feet per Minute (CFM) of airflow per ton of capacity. A 3-ton system requires 1,050 to 1,200 CFM of duct capacity.

Signs Your Ductwork Needs Replacement

  • Hot and Cold Spots: One bedroom is 78°F while another is 68°F, caused by disconnected or undersized duct runs.
  • Soaring Energy Bills: Conditioned air leaking into a 130°F attic wastes up to 30% of your HVAC energy.
  • Excessive Dust and Respiratory Irritation: Negative pressure in unsealed return ducts sucks insulation fibers, mold spores, and attic dust directly into living spaces.
  • Physical Damage or Age: Crushed flex ducts, rodent tunneling, moisture-degraded fiberglass, or ductwork older than 20–25 years.

Lifespan & Maintenance

Sheet metal trunk lines last 30 to 40+ years. Flexible branch ducts last 15 to 25 years before outer plastic jackets degrade and insulation compresses. Maintenance includes:

  • Inspecting duct connections in the attic annually for detached tape or sagging hangers.
  • Performing a "Duct Blaster" pressurization test during HVAC replacements to confirm leakage is below 6%.
  • Replacing pleated return air filters every 30 to 90 days to prevent dust accumulation on duct interiors.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I should clean or replace my ducts?

If your ducts are structurally sound sheet metal with minor surface dust, professional cleaning ($350–$600) is appropriate. However, if you have old flexible ducts that are collapsed, torn, mold-infested, or contaminated by rodent droppings, duct cleaning will tear the flexible lining and replacement is required.

How long does it take to replace ductwork in a home?

Replacing all ductwork in a standard 2,000 sq. ft. home typically takes a 2-person crew 1 to 2 full workdays (8 to 16 hours), depending on attic clearance and outdoor temperatures.

What is the difference between R-6 and R-8 duct insulation?

R-8 insulation is thicker and provides higher thermal resistance than R-6. Modern energy codes (IECC) in southern states require R-8 insulation for all ductwork installed in unconditioned attics to prevent condensation and reduce heat transfer.

Does replacing ductwork improve indoor air quality?

Yes, significantly. Sealing and replacing old leaky ducts prevents attic dust, fiberglass particles, pollen, and crawlspace moisture from being pulled into your home's breathing air.
