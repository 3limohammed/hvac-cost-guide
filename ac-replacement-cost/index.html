AC Replacement Cost: 2026 Central Air Guide

✓ Verified Pricing Turnkey AC Average: $4,000 – $7,800 Updated: 2026

The average cost to replace a central air conditioner (outdoor condenser and matched indoor evaporator coil) ranges from $4,000 to $7,800, with most homeowners spending approximately $5,600 for a standard 3-ton, 15.2 SEER2 system.

Average Cost of a 3-Ton AC Unit Installed

A 3-ton central air conditioner (36,000 BTU/hr) is the single most common residential cooling capacity installed in the United States, typically conditioning homes between 1,500 and 2,100 square feet. In 2026, the complete installed cost for a 3-ton system averages $6,200, with most homeowners paying between $4,800 and $8,500.

Can You Replace Just the Outside AC Compressor Unit?

When an outdoor unit fails, homeowners frequently ask if they can replace only the outdoor condenser while keeping their existing indoor evaporator coil. While physically possible in limited circumstances, contractors and manufacturers generally advise against it:

  • SEER2 Efficiency Mismatch: Under Department of Energy (DOE) regulations, systems must achieve certified AHRI matched ratings. An old indoor coil paired with a modern 15.2+ SEER2 condenser will fail to achieve its rated efficiency.
  • Voided Manufacturer Warranty: Most major brands (Carrier, Trane, Lennox) require a matched indoor coil to honor the 10-year parts warranty.
  • Refrigerant Incompatibility: If your older indoor unit was designed for phased-out R-22 or legacy R-410A oil, chemical contamination can destroy the new outdoor compressor within months.

Single-Stage vs. Two-Stage vs. Variable-Speed

The internal compressor technology directly dictates both initial price and ongoing electrical utility bills:

  • Single-Stage (Entry Level): Operates at 100% full capacity whenever turned on. Lowest upfront cost ($4,000–$5,800), but creates larger temperature swings and higher electrical draw.
  • Two-Stage (Mid-Tier): Runs at roughly 65% capacity on mild days and shifts to 100% during high-heat afternoons. Costs $1,200–$2,200 more, but enhances humidity removal and lowers monthly bills by 15%.
  • Variable-Speed Inverter (Premium): Automatically modulates between 25% and 100% capacity in 1% increments. Delivers whisper-quiet operation and 30%+ energy savings, but carries a premium price tag of $7,500–$12,000+.

Why You Must Replace the Evaporator Coil with the Condenser

Some contractors may offer to replace only the outdoor condenser unit to save $1,000–$1,800. Never do this. Mixing an old indoor coil with a modern condenser voids manufacturer warranties, decreases efficiency by up to 25%, and risks premature compressor burnout due to unmatched expansion valve (TXV) sizing and residual refrigerant oil incompatibilities.
