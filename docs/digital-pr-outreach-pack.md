### Template 2: AC Blowing Warm Air / Troubleshooting
* **Target Post:** *"AC running outside, fan spinning, but blowing lukewarm air inside. Help!"*
* **Copy-Paste Response:**

```markdown
Before calling a tech for a $150 diagnostic fee, check these 4 common culprits:

1. **Thermostat Setting:** Make sure it's set to **COOL** and the fan is set to **AUTO**, not "ON". If the fan is set to "ON", the blower constantly circulates unconditioned room air even when the outdoor compressor cycles off.
2. **Air Filter:** Pull your filter right now. If it’s caked in dust, airflow over the indoor evaporator coil drops, causing the coil to freeze solid into a block of ice. Look at the copper refrigerant line entering your air handler—is it frosted over? If yes, turn the system to OFF / Fan ON to thaw for 3-4 hours.
3. **Outside Condenser Compressor vs. Fan:** Go outside and listen. Is the big fan blade spinning, but the compressor itself is humming loudly or silent? That almost always means a blown dual-run capacitor ($15–$30 DIY part, or $150–$300 professional repair).
4. **Condensate Float Switch:** Check your indoor drain line pan. If it backed up with algae/water, the safety float switch tripped and cut 24V power to the outdoor condenser.

Here is a step-by-step diagnostic checklist covering voltages, capacitors, and coil inspections: [AC Blowing Warm Air Step-by-Step Diagnostic Guide](https://hvac-cost-guide.vercel.app/ac-blowing-warm-air/). Hope this saves you from an unnecessary service call!
```

### Template 3: Furnace Blowing Cold Air / Short Cycling
* **Target Post:** *"Furnace turns on for 5 seconds, flame lights, then clicks off and blows cold air"*
* **Copy-Paste Response:**

```markdown
If the burners ignite for 3 to 7 seconds and then immediately shut off, that is a textbook **dirty flame sensor** 90% of the time.

Here's what is happening:
When the gas ignites, the flame sensor needs to detect micro-amps of current through the flame to verify fire is present. Over 1–3 years, microscopic silica and carbon soot build an insulating layer on the metal probe. The control board thinks no flame ignited and cuts gas for safety.

**How to fix it yourself in 10 minutes:**
1. Turn off furnace power at the emergency wall switch.
2. Take off the upper burner door.
3. Locate the thin metal rod positioned directly in front of the last burner (usually held by one 1/4" hex screw with one wire attached).
4. Remove the screw, unplug the wire, and gently rub the metal rod with a piece of fine steel wool or an abrasive green Scotch-Brite pad (do NOT use sandpaper, as it leaves silica residue).
5. Wipe clean with a dry cloth and reinstall.

If that doesn't resolve it, check your error blink code through the sight glass on the blower door. Here is a full reference table of furnace flash codes and repair costs: [Furnace Blowing Cold Air Troubleshooting & Cost Guide](https://hvac-cost-guide.vercel.app/furnace-blowing-cold-air/).
```

### Template 4: Heat Pump vs Gas Furnace (Zone 4/5/6 Climates)
* **Target Post:** *"Replacing 20-year-old gas furnace. Should I switch to a heat pump or stick with natural gas?"*
* **Copy-Paste Response:**

```markdown
The answer in 2026 comes down to two factors: your local electric vs. gas rates ($/kWh vs $/therm) and winter design temperature.

1. **Cold-Climate Tech (ccASHP):** Modern variable-speed inverter heat pumps (Mitsubishi Hyper-Heating, Bosch IDS, Carrier Infinity) maintain 100% heating capacity down to 5°F and operate down to -15°F without relying on electric heat strips. You do NOT need a furnace backup unless you are in extreme Northern climates (North Dakota, Northern Maine).
2. **Dual-Fuel Setup (Best of Both Worlds):** If you already have natural gas service, pairing a heat pump with an 80% or 96% gas furnace is often the sweet spot. The heat pump handles heating down to ~35°F at ultra-high efficiency, and the gas furnace takes over during sub-freezing snaps.
3. **IRA Tax Incentives:** Qualifying heat pumps get up to a **$2,000 Federal Section 25C tax credit**, whereas gas furnaces only qualify for up to $600 (and must be 97%+ AFUE).

Check out this side-by-side cost and operating efficiency comparison: [Furnace vs. Heat Pump: 2026 Cold-Climate Breakdown](https://hvac-cost-guide.vercel.app/furnace-vs-heat-pump/).
```

### Template 5: Federal IRA Tax Credit Claiming (Section 25C)
* **Target Post:** *"How does the $2,000 heat pump tax credit actually work for 2026?"*
* **Copy-Paste Response:**

```markdown
Under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) Section 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit:

- **Amount:** 30% of total project cost (equipment + professional labor) up to a maximum cap of **$2,000 per year** for qualifying heat pumps and heat pump water heaters.
- **Form:** Filed using IRS Form 5695 on your annual federal tax return.
- **Eligibility Requirement:** It is NOT based on SEER alone anymore. The heat pump MUST meet the CEE (Consortium for Energy Efficiency) highest tier ratings for your region (North vs. South).
- **AHRI Certificate:** You must demand the AHRI (Air-Conditioning, Heating, and Refrigeration Institute) certificate from your installer matching the exact condenser + indoor coil combination. If the AHRI number doesn't qualify, the IRS can disallow the credit.

Here is a full breakdown of regional SEER2/HSPF2 minimum thresholds and contractor paperwork checklists: [2026 Heat Pump Tax Credits & Rebate Guide](https://hvac-cost-guide.vercel.app/heat-pump-tax-credits-2026/).
```

### Template 7: The "$5,000 Rule" (Repair vs Replace AC)
* **Target Post:** *"Compressor died on a 12-year-old AC. Contractor wants $2,200 to fix or $8,000 to replace. What should I do?"*
* **Copy-Paste Response:**

```markdown
Use the HVAC industry's **"$5,000 Rule"** to make this decision logically:

> **Multiply the age of the unit (in years) by the repair estimate ($).**
> - If the result is **less than $5,000**, repair it.
> - If the result is **greater than $5,000**, replace it.

In your situation:
12 years old × $2,200 repair cost = **$26,400** (Way above $5,000).

Why you should replace:
1. **Refrigerant Type:** A 12-year-old system likely uses phased-out R-410A or older R-22. Dropping $2,200 on an out-of-warranty compressor on an aging coil is putting good money into obsolete tech.
2. **Efficiency Gains:** Upgrading from an old 10-12 SEER unit to a new 15.2+ SEER2 unit can reduce your summer electric bills by 25% to 35%.
3. **Warranties:** New systems come with 10-year parts warranties, while repair jobs only offer 30–90 days on labor.

Here is a calculator and full decision matrix: [AC Repair vs. Replacement: The 2026 Decision Guide](https://hvac-cost-guide.vercel.app/repair-vs-replace-ac/).
```

### Template 8: How Often Should HVAC Be Serviced?
* **Target Post:** *"Is annual HVAC maintenance really necessary or just an upsell scam?"*
* **Copy-Paste Response:**

```markdown
As a general rule, an HVAC system should be inspected **twice a year**:
- **Spring:** AC tune-up (clean condenser coil, check refrigerant subcooling/superheat, test capacitor microfarads, clear condensate drain line).
- **Fall:** Heating tune-up (inspect heat exchanger for dangerous carbon monoxide hairline cracks, clean flame sensor, check gas manifold pressure).

**Is it a scam?**
Some corporate national chains use "$29 tune-ups" as aggressive sales funnels to scare homeowners into full system replacements. However, legit tune-ups ($80–$140) by independent contractors are worth it for two reasons:
1. **Manufacturer Warranty Compliance:** Carrier, Trane, Lennox, and Goodman fine print states warranties can be voided if damage is caused by lack of documented maintenance.
2. **Preventing $300 emergency calls:** A weakening $20 capacitor will fail on the hottest 95°F day of the year, usually taking the expensive motor with it.

Check out this DIY maintenance checklist vs what requires a licensed technician: [HVAC Maintenance Frequency & DIY Checklist](https://hvac-cost-guide.vercel.app/how-often-should-hvac-be-serviced/).
```

### Template 9: Replacing Ductwork Costs & When It's Needed
* **Target Post:** *"HVAC guy says my ductwork needs full replacement. Quoted $5,500. Legit?"*
* **Copy-Paste Response:**

```markdown
Ductwork replacement costs **$12 to $25 per linear foot** installed, averaging **$2,500 to $5,200** for a full 1,800–2,200 sq. ft. home in an accessible attic or crawlspace. $5,500 is within normal range if it includes:
- Removing old fiberglass/mylar flexible duct runs.
- Installing properly sized R-8 insulated flex ducts or rigid sheet metal trunk lines.
- Aeroseal or mastic paste sealing at all boots and plenums.

**Do you actually need it?**
Unless the ducting is crushed, infested with mold/rodents, or severely undersized for your new unit (causing high static pressure), you often only need professional mastic sealing ($800–$1,500), not total replacement. Ask the contractor for static pressure readings (inches of water column) before agreeing.
```
