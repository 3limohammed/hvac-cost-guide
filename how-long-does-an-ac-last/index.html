How Long Does an Air Conditioner Last?

✓ Lifespan Research National Median Lifespan: 12 – 17 Years Coastal Lifespan: 8 – 12 Years

The national average lifespan of a residential central air conditioner is 12 to 17 years. However, geography and operating climate are the single largest determining factors: systems in coastal Florida rarely exceed 10 years, while systems in the Great Lakes region often run for 18+ years.

4 Reasons Central ACs Fail Prematurely

  1. Dirty Air Filters: A clogged 1-inch fiberglass filter starves the indoor blower of return airflow. Static pressure spikes, causing the evaporator coil to freeze into a solid block of ice and forcing liquid refrigerant back into the compressor (liquid slugging), destroying internal valves.
  2. Acid & Refrigerant Moisture: If an installer fails to pull a deep vacuum below 500 microns when brazing line sets, atmospheric moisture combines with POE synthetic oil to form hydrochloric and hydrofluoric acids that corrode internal motor insulation.
  3. Oversized Equipment: Units sized larger than Manual J calculations short-cycle (turn on and off every 8 minutes). Frequent starts place enormous electrical stress on capacitors and compressor windings.
  4. Neglected Outdoor Coil Cleaning: Grass clippings, pet hair, and pollen blanket outdoor condenser coils, blocking heat rejection and forcing the compressor to run at dangerously high discharge pressures.
